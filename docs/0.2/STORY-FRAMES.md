# A complete moment on one screen

Reader.8 made each workflow's illustration different but kept it fixed through
the entire recording. It also validated support scenes after scrolling to them.
Both decisions missed the intended experience.

- [ ] Give the viewport an explicit budget: header, short title, diagram, illustrated moment, navigation.
  - [ ] Fit desktop, tablet, mobile, and short mobile viewports without page scrolling.
  - [ ] Keep every illustration contained, with no crop or hidden overflow used to fake a fit.
  - [ ] Keep the diagram's people, objects and arrows; use a compact mobile arrangement.
  - [ ] Keep the same diagram and navigation positions while stepping.
- [ ] Direct the narrative from the observed events.
  - [ ] Select a representative frame for each action and its actual outcome.
  - [ ] Show proposal preparation, relay and receipt as different actions.
  - [ ] Show agreement, reservation, readiness and settlement as different actions.
  - [ ] Keep refusals, retries, missing evidence and rollback distinct from success.
  - [ ] Connect live inspection and authored diagram selections to relevant images.
  - [ ] Preserve all recorded evidence; public chapters follow the main handoffs.
- [ ] Validate the journey as a reader.
  - [ ] Start at the top, click the actual carousel, and capture the entire viewport.
  - [ ] Check every moment at desktop and mobile sizes, without scrolling into view.
  - [ ] Confirm source quotations, golden comparisons and command authorization remain intact.
  - [ ] Save the storyboard, generation provenance and visual review; commit and push.

The upper infographic explains relationships and observed state. The lower frame
shows what someone just did and what that enabled or prevented. Two short lines
provide the names and exact consequence. A refusal depicts the blocked attempt;
it must not imply that the corresponding successful action occurred.

Original documents and source code remain reading surfaces with their own panes.
Workflow presentations fit on screen. Print and no-JavaScript chapters retain
normal document flow. Large accessibility text may use document flow rather than
clipping content or reducing the requested text size.
